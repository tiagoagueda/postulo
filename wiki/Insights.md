# Insights

**Insights**, in the navigation.

What the record adds up to: how far applications get, how many are answered at all, how
long employers take, and which sources actually produce something.

## Read from the timeline, not from statuses

This is the distinction that makes the page worth having.

An application that reached an interview and was then rejected has a current status of
*rejected*. Counting current statuses would report that you have had no interviews —
which is both wrong and, after a long search, quietly demoralising.

Every figure here comes from the event log instead. A stage counts applications that
**ever reached it**, whatever happened afterwards. Three final-round rejections show as
three interviews, because that is what they were.

## How far things got

A funnel from *Applied* down to *Offer*. Each row shows how many applications ever
reached that stage and what share of everything you sent that represents.

Read it for the shape rather than the numbers. A funnel that narrows sharply between
*Applied* and *Acknowledged* says something different from one that narrows between
*Interviewing* and *Offer* — the first is a problem with how applications are being sent,
the second is a problem with what happens once you are in the room.

## Got any reply

The share of applications that produced **any** answer.

A rejection counts as a reply. Being turned down is information, and an employer who
says no has behaved better than one who says nothing. Only silence counts against the
figure, which is why *Ghosted* is a status in its own right.

## How long they take

The **median** number of days between applying and the first sign of life, with the
fastest and slowest alongside it.

Median rather than average, deliberately: one employer who answers after four months
would drag an average somewhere no real employer sits. The median tells you what to
expect; the slowest tells you what is possible.

Applications still waiting are counted separately. They have no reply time yet, and
treating them as instant or as infinite would both be wrong.

## Interviews

The median number of days between applying and the first interview, how many
applications reached one, and what is in the diary. Read from the timeline's *interview*
entries — an interview typed in by hand counts as much as one settled from the diary — and
the kinds of interview held are tallied from the diary itself.

## Where they came from

A table comparing sources: how many you sent, how many were answered, how many reached
an interview, how many produced an offer.

A *Quiet* column counts the applications from each source that are still live but have
gone quiet — silent past your threshold, with nothing planned. They are not failures yet,
but they are not replies either, and a source whose applications mostly go quiet is telling
you something the response rate has not caught up with. Beneath the table, the companies
where applications have gone quiet are named.

This is the figure most likely to change what you do, and it depends entirely on filling
in **found via** when you record a posting. Everything without one is grouped as *Not
recorded*, which is honest but not useful. Two minutes of discipline here is worth more
than the rest of the page.

## Small numbers

A job search produces dozens of applications, not thousands. Below five, Postulo says so
rather than quietly presenting a percentage that is really one story with a decimal point
attached.

Even above five, treat these as a record of what happened rather than as a rate that
predicts anything. Nine applications and one interview is not an eleven per cent
interview rate; it is one interview.
