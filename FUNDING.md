# Supporting Postulo

**Voluntary support is this project's only income.** Not its main one — its only one.
There is no company behind Postulo, no funding round, no investor, no paid tier waiting to
appear once enough people depend on it. One person writes it, and what it costs to keep
going comes from people who decided it was worth something.

<a href="https://buymeacoffee.com/tiagoagueda"><img src="assets/support/buy-me-a-coffee.png" alt="Buy me a coffee" height="44"></a>

This file exists because `.github/FUNDING.yml` can hold a link and nothing else, and a link
on its own does not say what is being asked for, what it changes, or what it does not.

## What it will never buy

This is the part that matters most, so it comes first.

**No feature of Postulo is, or ever will be, behind a paywall.** People looking for work
are, more often than not, people who cannot afford to pay for the tools to find it. That is
the whole reason this project exists, and it is not a marketing position that can be
revisited when the numbers get interesting. Concretely, and permanently:

- No paid tier, no "pro" edition, no licence key, no enterprise build.
- No feature that works for a while and then asks for money.
- No limit on records, documents, storage, accounts or exports that money removes.
- No feature held back from the open repository and sold separately.
- **Nothing inside the application will ever ask you for money** — no banner, no prompt, no
  reminder on the dashboard, no anniversary email. The README, this file and the wiki are
  the only places the question is put.

The AGPL guarantees this for the code that exists. These paragraphs are the promise about
the code that has not been written yet, and `CONTRIBUTING.md` says a pull request that
breaks it will be declined however good it is.

**Money does not buy influence, either.** It does not move an issue up the list, it does
not get a feature built, and it does not carry more weight in a design argument than a
well-written bug report from somebody who has given nothing. A donor and a stranger get the
same answer to the same question. If that is disappointing, it is meant to be: a project
that sells priority has quietly become a product with customers, and its roadmap stops
belonging to the people who use it.

## What it actually pays for

Postulo is developed on a self-hosted Forgejo instance and mirrored to GitHub, with a
public test instance kept running so that anybody can see the thing working before
installing it. That means real, recurring costs — a domain, a machine that stays on,
bandwidth, backups, and the certificates and monitoring that keep them honest.

And it pays, partly, for the time. This is a full-time-shaped amount of work being done
outside a full-time job. Support does not make it a salary; it makes it sustainable enough
to keep choosing.

No breakdown of amounts is published here, because publishing one implies a threshold, and
a threshold implies something happens when it is missed. Nothing happens when it is missed.
The project is AGPL and self-hostable precisely so that it does not depend on this file
working.

## If money is the wrong thing to give

For a lot of people who need Postulo, it is. These are worth as much, and some of them are
worth more:

- **Report a bug.** Especially an unglamorous one. Several of the most useful fixes in this
  project began as somebody saying "this looks wrong on my machine" — the flags on Windows,
  the icons in the settings sidebar, the QR code in the dark theme. None of those would have
  been found by testing; they needed eyes on a different screen.
- **Review a translation.** Postulo speaks every official language of the European Union,
  and a machine-assisted draft carries a `draft` flag until a speaker has read it.
  Reviewing your own language is the single highest-value contribution available, and it
  needs no code. See
  [docs/TRANSLATING.md](docs/TRANSLATING.md).
- **Try it with a screen reader, a keyboard only, or scripts turned off**, and say what
  broke. The test suite runs axe-core over every page it visits, which catches what a
  machine can catch and nothing else.
- **Tell one person who needs this that it exists.** Someone job-hunting and keeping track
  in a spreadsheet is exactly who this was built for, and they have no way of knowing.
- **Write documentation**, or fix the sentence that confused you. The person who was
  confused is the only one who knows which sentence it was.

## For organisations

If your company, university or employment service runs Postulo internally — you are welcome
to, that is what the licence is for, and nothing here changes.

Two things worth saying plainly. First, the AGPL means that if you run a **modified**
Postulo as a network service, the people using it are entitled to your source; that is a
condition of the licence and not a request. Second, an organisation running this on staff
time is getting something a single unpaid person maintains, and the honest ways to keep
that arrangement working are to fund it, to contribute the fixes you make rather than
carrying them privately, or both.

There is no support contract, no SLA and no invoice, because there is nobody to answer a
pager. If your use needs those, self-hosting and funding the upstream is the arrangement
that is actually on offer.

## Where the money goes, exactly

To Tiago Águeda, through [Buy Me a Coffee](https://buymeacoffee.com/tiagoagueda). There is
no fund, no foundation, no collective, and nobody else with a claim on it. Contributors are
credited in the history and the changelog; none of them are paid, and none of them have been
asked to work for free on the understanding that money might arrive.

## Questions this file should answer

**Is a one-off better than a monthly amount?** Monthly is more useful, because it is what
makes a recurring cost predictable. A one-off is genuinely welcome and nothing about it is
worth less.

**Can I get an invoice or a receipt?** Buy Me a Coffee issues its own receipt. Anything
beyond that — a company invoice, a VAT number, a purchase-order reference — is a
conversation: <tiago.agueda@tiagoagueda.com>.

**Will supporting get my issue looked at sooner?** No. See above; that is deliberate.

**Can I sponsor a specific feature?** Not as a transaction. If something matters enough to
you to pay for it, open an issue and make the case, because the case is what moves it — and
if the case is good it gets built whether or not any money appears.

**I would rather give to someone who needs it more.** Then do that. Postulo exists for
people looking for work, and someone in that position needs it more than this project does.
